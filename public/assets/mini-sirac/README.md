# Mini Sıraç crew

Generated on 2026-10-13 in the user's Google Flow account with Omni 1.1 Flash,
using the public portfolio portrait as the explicit identity reference.

Flow project: https://flow.google.com/project/06b2c361-1b54-4f01-8c6b-79685a5ff244

Four original 720p, 6-second clips are retained in the user's Downloads folder:
- Man_walking_in_place_20261013012831.mp4
- Man_pushing_invisible_panel_20261013011109.mp4
- Man_pulling_invisible_rope_20261013011139.mp4
- Man_carrying_invisible_shelf_ani…_20261013011156.mp4

The shipped WebP atlases contain 144 frames at 24 fps, laid out in a 12 × 12 grid.
Each frame is 240 pixels high; push/pull/carry/walk widths are 156/144/112/132 pixels.
Green backgrounds are keyed and despilled during video processing, then cropped
to the character's full movement bounds. No face replacement is applied.

The source prompts request the same face, short dark hair, charcoal blazer,
black shirt, dark trousers and black shoes; locked side-view camera; a centered
full body; green background; natural steps in place; and no audio or camera cuts.
Actions: push an invisible panel with both palms at chest height; pull toward the
right while gripping to the left; carry an invisible shelf with raised palms.

The site animates inert visual copies of real interface elements. React's live
layout is revealed after the short intro; Escape/Skip, resize, asset failures and
reduced-motion preference all release the stage and its input restrictions.

The normal arms-down walk is used for departures. Workers turn and walk beyond
the viewport boundary at full opacity; the stage has no final dissolve.
Contact tracks are generated by scripts/track-mini-crew.py. Palms and shoes
are tracked per frame to preserve hand contact and a stable floor. Gait cycles
advance with traveled distance; travel paths and speed are capped so acceleration
and stopping do not cause a constant-speed walk to slide across the screen.
The walk plate uses foreground segmentation to keep green spill from cutting
holes in the dark suit.

On 2026-10-13, release-and-turn plates were generated with explicit first and
last frames. The first frame is the exact held carry/push pose; the end frame
faces the opposite direction with arms down. Selected sources:
- Man_walking_in_place_20261013014154.mp4 (push release)
- Man_lowering_arms_and_walking_20261013014257.mp4 (carry release)
Initial attempts with ambiguous turns are retained in Downloads but not shipped.

The runtime uses these continuous plates for lowering the arms and the physical
half turn, then repeats matching walking frames from the same plate. No squash
turn or direct raised-arm-to-walk substitution remains. Head, torso and floor
tracking preserve size and position across the transition. Only navbar/portrait
use two-person teams; other pieces use one person. Departures return to the
original edge, keeping left and right crews apart. Once all pieces are placed,
the actual page becomes interactive while remaining workers walk out. Scrolling,
Skip, Escape during assembly, resize and pagehide dispose of the stage.

The opening is blank, including during asset loading. All staged pieces start
fully outside the viewport. Departure travel starts on the first walking step
(push frame 58, carry frame 68), before the later seamless-loop boundary.
Walking frames and body travel share the source 24 fps cadence.

Mobile (up to 768px) uses one pusher in the portrait gutter. Navigation, title,
details and actions reveal within the first two seconds; only the portrait is
transported. The reserved right gutter keeps arrival and departure clear of
text and buttons. The replay control is an icon with an accessible label.
